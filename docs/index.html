Read the paper Follow the argument

Central claim

Fluent generation is not dependable autonomy.
Reliability emerges from explicit state, evidence, action contracts, verification, and proportionate governance.

The paper asks one question across the whole stack: what must surround a language model before it can move from producing a response to choosing and verifying actions over time?

Reader map

From source to accountable action

The paper follows a progression from grounding information to acting under authority, then asks what evidence and safeguards make that action credible.

01 • Ground

Retrieve and attribute evidence. RAG lets a system work over current, scoped, and attributable information, but does not guarantee factuality by itself.

02 • Decide

Represent state and choose the next step. Planning, memory, and tools turn a language model into a component of a control loop.

03 • Act

Constrain external authority. Tool contracts, permissions, budgets, and validators determine whether a proposed action can happen.

04 • Account

Measure outcomes and side effects. Evaluation must cover success, recovery, cost, traceability, and safety together.

The practical rule

Use the least autonomy that solves the task.

Use RAG for attributable evidence, workflows for predictable control, bounded agents for uncertain but verifiable work, and multi-agent systems only when distinct roles create a measurable advantage.
