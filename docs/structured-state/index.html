Can Structured State Unify Agentic Execution?

Mehrdad Zakershahrak, Samira Ghodratnama, Mohammadali Karfarma

September 2026 · Research preprint · 36 pages

Read the full paper (PDF)

Abstract

Context management, coordination, recovery, and verification are often treated as separate problems in agentic systems, although each depends on goals, evidence, authority, effects, and unresolved work. We ask whether a common representation of these distinctions can unify agentic execution. We introduce the typed execution case, a persistent, versioned state model with explicit execution relationships, and characterize nineteen structural operation families over it. A common contract framework distinguishes information preservation from authorized state change and yields conditional properties for dependency-closed views, authority non-amplification, and composition. An executable reference model provides 55 passing contract tests, including 6 composed scenarios. A controlled study spanning all nineteen families and seven operation pairs records 3,168 executions satisfying their specified outcome checks. In stress workloads, compaction, projection, and retrieval reduce delivered context by 86.3%, 88.0%, and 77.7%, respectively; caching reduces compute calls by 72.5%, and selective invalidation and merge reduce their respective computation and worker calls by 45.0%. Authorization and completion checks retain correct decisions in all tested cases. Gains vary by workload, and some operations incur additional cost. A separate storage study recovers correctly across 192 trials, including 144 abrupt interruptions. These results establish benefits of individual operations and preservation properties within the tested implementations. A pilot using a model on four tasks yields no fully successful tasks in either arm, leaving improvements in completion by interactive agents and advantages over matched alternative state representations open. The framework thus provides a common, executable basis for studying both the benefits and the limits of structural operations.
